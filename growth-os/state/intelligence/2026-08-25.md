## Thread 1: Grant Thornton 2026 AI Impact Survey (governance-audit confidence gap)

Two WebSearch queries surfaced a genuinely new, primary-sourced anxiety anchor: Grant Thornton's 2026 AI Impact Survey. Verified by direct WebFetch of the report's own page (`grantthornton.com/services/advisory-services/artificial-intelligence/2026-ai-impact-survey`), re-fetched a second time with a neutral, non-leading prompt ("list every statistic verbatim") specifically to rule out the WebFetch model echoing my search-query framing back rather than reading the real page, the same discipline this swarm applied 2026-08-20 after catching two unverifiable WRITER figures. Both fetches returned identical figures.

**Methodology (confirmed, both fetches agree):** survey of 950 business leaders (senior leadership including CEOs, CFOs, COOs, CIOs/CTOs and their direct reports) across 10 industries (asset management, banking, construction/real estate, energy, insurance, manufacturing, media/entertainment, private equity, services, technology/telecom, ~100 per industry except banking at 50), fielded 2026-02-23 to 2026-03-18. Functional split: operations 390, finance 313, IT 234, CEO/managing partner 13.

**Key figures, in order of appearance on the page:**
1. 78% of business executives lack strong confidence they could pass an independent AI governance audit within 90 days.
2. Organizations with fully integrated AI are nearly 4x more likely to report revenue growth than those still piloting (58% vs 15%).
3. 78% of executives name governance as the function most needing focus to meet their AI ambitions, even though 46% cite governance failures as a leading cause of underperformance.
4. 75% of boards have approved major AI investments, yet 48% have not set AI governance expectations and 46% have not integrated AI risk.
5. **Only 7% of organizations still piloting AI are very confident they could pass an independent AI governance audit within 90 days, versus 74% of organizations with fully integrated AI.** (The sharpest, most usable figure: a direct maturity-gap number, not just a flat 78%.)
6. 77% of operations leaders do not have a fully developed, implemented AI strategy.
7. Only 6% of CIOs/CTOs and only 1% of COOs say their workforce is fully ready to adopt AI.
8. Nearly 3 in 4 organizations give agentic AI access to data/processes; only 20% have a tested AI incident response plan.
9. Only 5% allow agents to execute high-stakes decisions without human review; 60% limit agents to moderate-risk task automation.
10. 54% of COOs are concerned about regulatory/compliance uncertainty for agentic AI vs. 20% of CIOs/CTOs.
