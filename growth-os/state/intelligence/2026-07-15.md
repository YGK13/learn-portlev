## Thread 1: AMS and Mercer stats in lesson 04 re-verified against primary sources, one confirmed, one mis-scoped, one UNVERIFIABLE

The drafted lesson (`content/tracks/ai-foundations/04-is-ai-going-to-take-my-executive-job.mdx`, still `status: draft`) carries four stats sourced through Reworked.co's secondary reporting. Each was traced to its primary source today.

**80% (CHROs say AI is essential to keeping talent pools competitive): CONFIRMED, correctly scoped.**
Primary source: [AMS, "New AMS Research Reveals Growing Tension Between C-Suite and HR Leaders as AI Transforms Hiring," published 2026-01-29](https://www.weareams.com/news/new-ams-research-reveals-growing-tension-between-c-suite-and-hr-leaders-as-ai-transforms-hiring/), also carried on [Morningstar/BusinessWire](https://www.morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20260129418874/new-ams-research-reveals-growing-tension-between-c-suite-and-hr-leaders-as-ai-transforms-hiring). Survey conducted by Wakefield Research, 300 total decision-makers (100 CHROs, 100 other C-suite leaders, 100 HR and talent decision-makers), fielded 2025-10-09 to 2025-10-28. Exact finding: "64% of all respondents believe their organization's talent pool will not remain competitive without AI, jumping to 80% of CHROs." The lesson's `<Stat>` wording ("80% of CHROs say AI is essential...") matches this correctly: 80% is the CHRO subgroup figure, not the full 300-person sample. No change needed. One precision note for the citation line: the lesson's source tag reads "AMS Survey, January 2026, n=300 CHROs and HR leaders"; more precise would be "n=300 decision-makers (100 CHROs), fielded Oct 2025, published Jan 2026," since the 80% figure is the 100-CHRO subgroup, not all 300.

**89% ("have not deployed AI across major HR functions"): REAL NUMBER, MIS-SCOPED in the draft.**
Same AMS/Wakefield press release, exact finding: "89% of companies say they are not using AI across all major talent acquisition functions." The draft's paragraph text says "89% have not deployed AI across major HR functions" (Context section, paragraph 1). This broadens a talent-acquisition-specific finding into a claim about HR functions generally, which the source does not support. This is the same category of error caught in the 2026-07-14 brief on the SHRM stat (real number, overstated scope). **Fix needed before publish:** narrow the wording to "major recruiting/talent-acquisition functions," not "major HR functions."

**13% (of employees have heard from HR about AI): CONFIRMED, correct.**
Primary source: [Mercer newsroom, "Leadership vacuum fuels tech anxiety in the workplace, according to Mercer"](https://www.mercer.com/about/newsroom/leadership-vacuum-fuels-tech-anxiety-in-the-workplace-according-to-mercer/) and the underlying report [Mercer, "HR Technology's Impact on the Workforce"](https://www.mercer.com/en-us/insights/people-strategy/hr-transformation/hr-technologys-impact-on-the-workforce/). Survey: over 8,500 employees globally, fielded 2025-03-17 to 2025-03-31. Exact quote: "only 13% of workers have heard from HR on the topic" (AI's impact on their job or the business). Matches the draft. No change needed.

**78% ("of knowledge workers are already using AI tools at work"): NOT Mercer, and not a clean match to any single real statistic. Mis-attributed, not a clean fabrication.**
This is the important finding of the run, corrected during audit (auditor: `audit/intelligence`, same day). The draft pairs 78% with the 13% Mercer figure in both the body text (Step 3) and a `<Stat>` block, citing "Mercer 2025 HR Tech Report (cited in Reworked.co, 2026)." Neither the Mercer newsroom release, the underlying Mercer report page, nor multiple targeted web searches turned up a 78%-employees-using-AI figure anywhere in Mercer's 2025 HR Technology's Impact on the Workforce research. The closest adjacent figures in that report are region-specific (UAE ~85%, Mexico ~72% of workers have used AI at work) and a separate confidence figure (under 30% trust their org to guide their next skills). So the 78% is confirmed NOT a Mercer figure. **Audit correction:** the original run's language ("possible hallucination") overstated the uncertainty. Fetching the Reworked.co article directly (not just search snippets) shows its "78%" is itself a hyperlink, targeting AIHR's "HR Trends Report 2025" PDF, not Mercer. Tracing further: Microsoft and LinkedIn's 2024 Work Trend Index Annual Report (Edelman Data & Intelligence, 31,000 full-time knowledge workers, 31 markets, fielded 2024-02-15 to 2024-03-28, published 2024-05-08, [microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/ai-at-work-is-here-now-comes-the-hard-part](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/ai-at-work-is-here-now-comes-the-hard-part)) reports two distinct, real, dated figures that both round to "78ish": "75% of global knowledge workers" now use AI at work, and separately "78% of AI users are bringing their own AI tools to work (BYOAI)" (a different denominator: percent of AI users, not percent of all workers). Reworked.co/AIHR's "78% of knowledge workers are already using AI tools at work" appears to conflate these two into a single incorrect population. **Net result: no primary source supports "78% of knowledge workers already using AI tools at work" as literally worded.** This stat should not ship as currently sourced or worded. Two real fix paths, not one: (a) cite the correct, verified Microsoft/LinkedIn figure instead: "75% of knowledge workers already use AI at work" (Microsoft/LinkedIn 2024 Work Trend Index, published 2024-05-08, over two years old by today so flag as slightly dated but real and primary-traceable), paired with the confirmed 13% Mercer figure as two separately-cited stats, not one blended citation; or (b) drop the adoption number and let the paragraph stand on the confirmed 13% figure alone. Do not keep the current Mercer-attributed 78%.

Search attempts made and their results, for the audit trail: WebSearch for the exact Reworked.co/Mercer 78%/13% pairing (2 queries); WebFetch of the Mercer newsroom press release (full text, 13% confirmed, 78% absent); WebFetch of the Mercer "HR Technology's Impact on the Workforce" report page (full text, 78% absent, closest is UAE 85% / Mexico 72%); WebSearch for "78% of employees already using AI" paired with any HR-guidance figure (surfaced unrelated stats: 89% of AI users bring their own tools to work per one blog aggregator, 78% of HR leaders say AI made their workforce more innovative per CNBC, neither matches the draft's claim or context). **Audit follow-up (same day):** WebFetch of Reworked.co directly showed the "78%" hyperlink targets AIHR's HR Trends Report 2025 PDF (image-based, not machine-readable); WebSearch traced the number to Microsoft/LinkedIn's 2024 Work Trend Index (75% adoption + 78% BYOAI-among-users, confirmed via microsoft.com/worklab and independently corroborated by GeekWire's 2024-05-08 coverage).
